# Tabdock's page link and MCP-B's local relay

Both projects let an MCP client call tools a web page registers on `document.modelContext`, the page dialling out to a relay over a WebSocket. MCP-B's `@mcp-b/webmcp-local-relay` speaks `webmcp.v1` for one person on one machine; Tabdock's `tabdock.v1` serves several people and clients on one page, approved by its operator, through a relay that may be public. This comparison on nine axes (M5 decision D4, ADR 0031) grounds the upstream proposal in `docs/upstream/mcp-b.md`.

Every claim about MCP-B was checked on 5 October 2026 against the 6.0 beta, `6.0.0-beta.20261001010549`, which is still npm's `beta` while `latest` is 5.1.0. Citations: **R** is the relay's `dist/mcpRelayServer-DEgebuO2.mjs` with its line number, **C** its `dist/cli.mjs`, **E** and **W** its `dist/browser/embed.js` and `widget.js` (one line each, so cited by the code quoted), **RM** its README, **D** the reference at docs.mcp-b.ai/packages/webmcp-local-relay/reference, and **B** our own measurement in `docs/notes/baseline.md` (`baseline.raw.mcpb6-beta.json`). Tabdock's side cites SPEC.md and its ADRs.

**Transport and framing.** MCP-B's `embed.js` fetches `widget.html` from beside itself and loads it as a hidden iframe from a blob URL, so it inherits the host page's origin [E; RM, "How it connects"]. The iframe owns the WebSocket and talks to the page by `postMessage` addressed to the page's own origin [E; W]. The relay accepts three subprotocols, `webmcp.v1` for pages, `webmcp-discovery.v1` and `webmcp-relay.v1` between relays [R507]; frames are JSON keyed by `type` [R365, R439], checked with zod on the relay [R916] and by hand in the widget [W], up to 10,000,000 bytes [R591]. A page sends its whole tool list on every change [R373, R956]. In Tabdock the adapter, inside the page, owns the socket; frames are `{t}` objects, zod-validated on both sides, capped at 1 MB, with results cut at 120,000 characters (SPEC section 6).

**Who dials whom.** In both the page dials out. MCP-B's widget scans `127.0.0.1` and `[::1]` from port 9333 to 9348, 1.2 s per probe, keeping the last endpoint in `sessionStorage`, and refuses a relay host that is not loopback ("relayHost must be a loopback address") [W]. The MCP client launches the relay as a stdio child [C; R1540], and a second relay process joins the first in client mode [R642, R685]. A Tabdock page dials the one relay URL its operator configured, `ws` on loopback or `wss`, and MCP clients reach `/mcp` over Streamable HTTP, locally or from Claude's cloud (SPEC sections 2 and 7, ADRs 0014 and 0022).

**Trust boundaries and auth.** MCP-B binds `127.0.0.1` by default, but `--host` binds elsewhere [R584, R1434] and the README only advises loopback "unless you add a separate trusted boundary" [RM]. Page origins default to `*` [R590, R1429; D]. The relay judges a page by its `Origin` header when there is one and otherwise by the origin the page claims in `hello` [R932; D], and refuses `webmcp-relay.v1` only to a socket that sends an `Origin` [R801], so any local process that omits the header can join as a relay client and list and invoke every tool [R986, R1005]. The MCP side has no authentication; stdio makes it the user's own process. Tabdock takes the origin from the header alone and refuses a socket without one (S1), enforces an allowlist (S2), checks `Host` and `Origin` on `/mcp` (S12), and serves only authenticated users (a signed-in account, or local mode's token holder), who attach with a single-use pairing code or an invite the operator minted, always under the operator's approval or policy (S3, S4).

**Multi-user and approval.** MCP-B answers `hello/accepted` as soon as the origin passes [R942, R947]: no operator approval, no roles, no prompts, and one implicit user; extra MCP clients share the first relay [RM, "Client mode"]. Tabdock attaches nobody without the operator's approval on the page (S4), enforces roles in relay and adapter (S5), prompts on the page before a consequential call unless the page opted in to confirmation in the client (S6), runs writes one at a time (section 5), and cancels in-flight calls on revoke (S8).

**Tool naming and listing.** MCP-B registers each page tool as an MCP tool under its name sanitized to `[a-zA-Z0-9_]`, at most 128 characters, adding a four-character tab id when two tabs share a name and `_2` and onwards for later collisions [R110, R140, R297]. Three static tools sit beside them: `webmcp_list_sources`, `webmcp_list_tools` and `webmcp_open_page`, which opens any http or https URL in the default browser or reloads a connected tab [R1571, R1616]. Tools that require task execution are left out [R363]. Tabdock always serves five fixed tools, and from M5, behind a flag, lists members' page tools as `<page id>__<tool>`, by role, at most 64 characters (ADR 0025).

**Results and errors.** MCP-B's embed parses the runtime's text: an MCP `CallToolResult` passes through, so the page chooses content types and `isError`, a JSON object also becomes structured content, and `input_required` is refused [E; D]. The relay checks arguments against `inputSchema` before forwarding [R1746; B] and the result against the SDK's schema after [R1364]. Measured on 6, a plain string reached the client unquoted, a handler's error read `Tool execution failed`, and of the page's annotations only `readOnlyHint` arrived [B]. Tabdock forwards the runtime's text under its header, so on 6 a string arrives quoted (ADR 0031), sends a JSON object as that labelled text alone, never as structured content, which a client may show its model in place of the label (ADR 0025's notes), and answers failures with coded errors (SPEC section 7).

**Untrusted content.** MCP-B prefixes each description with `[WebMCP <tab id> • <title>]`, where the title is the page's own document title [R1772; W], and passes the page's title and description through as written [R1748]. Results carry no label, and `untrustedContentHint` did not reach the client [B]. Tabdock labels every result untrusted, caps page text, and puts a first-class tool's quoted description behind a relay prefix that calls it untrusted (S10).

**Lifetime and sleep.** MCP-B's relay pings every 15 s and closes a page that has not answered for over 25 s, at the next ping, about 30 s after its last answer, since the check runs on the ping's own timer [R511, R1074; D]; a closed socket removes the tab's tools and fails its calls [R1056]. The widget retries after about 500 ms, rescans after 10, 20 and 30 s, then probes every two minutes [W; RM]. Calls time out after 65 s at the relay [R592] and 60 s in the widget [W]. There is no cancel frame [R439]: the relay's tool handler ignores the request's signal [R1757] and the embed passes none to `executeTool` [E], so a cancelled or timed-out call runs on in the page. A Tabdock page that drops is asleep for a 10 minute resume window and resumes with its token; `cancel` aborts the handler's signal; deliberate detach and supersession have close codes; and the adapter holds a Web Lock while attached (SPEC sections 6 and 8).

**Versioning.** The subprotocol is `webmcp.v1` and the relay's `server-hello` says `version: 1` [R411, R507], yet 5.1.0's embed sends input as a JSON string and 6's as an object [E; B], so embed and runtime must share a major [D; RM]. Both majors' widgets ignore a frame type they do not know [W, and 5.1.0's `widget.html`]. Tabdock names its link `tabdock.v1`, sends `hello.v` and `adapterVersion`, and ignores unknown types after `hello` (SPEC section 6, ADR 0023).

## What follows

The shapes rhyme: hello, full tool-list replacement, invoke and result, ping. The gaps are Tabdock's security work: an `Origin` the page cannot claim, an explicit allowlist, authenticated clients, an approval on the page, roles and cancellation. MCP-B's defaults suit one machine, where the user's own processes are the boundary, and would break S1, S2 and S4 on a public relay. So the proposal starts with the gap that hurts local users today, a `cancel` frame older widgets ignore, and offers a remote profile only on a subprotocol of its own that keeps those rules.
