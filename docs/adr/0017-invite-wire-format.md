# 0017: Invite wire format and clarifications of ADR 0016

Status: Accepted by the owner, 3 October 2026. Changes SPEC sections 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 (S14), and settles questions ADR 0016 left open.

## Context

ADR 0016 accepted signed-in invites for M4 but fixed no wire format, and the page link cannot carry one: `attach_request` allows only `via` `code` or `qr`, roster entries have no kind or end, `PolicySchema` has no `invites`, `invite_required` is not an error code, and the single-use store knows only the kind `pair` (`docs/notes/m4/invites-map.md`). ADR 0016 also leaves open which member sponsors, how long "while the page is open" lasts, what a member's redemption means, and what a control invite does when the driver seat is taken, which the relay today settles by silently granting observer (`hub.ts:1338-1349`); nor does the relay check a granted role against anything but `maxDrivers` (`hub.ts:1294`).

## Decision

**Frames.** `invite_create` (page to relay) carries `inviteId`, drawn by the adapter so it can store its record before the relay answers; `role` (`observer` for Can watch, `driver` for Can control); `label`, 1 to 60 characters; `uses`, 1 to 20 for watch and exactly 1 for control; `expiresAt` or null; and `secretHash`, 64 hex characters. `invite_cancel` (page to relay) carries `inviteId`. `invites` (relay to page, after each `welcome` and on every change) carries `linkBase` (`<public URL>/i`, or null without a public URL, when minting is refused), at most 10 live invites with uses left, sponsor, a pending flag and a refusal count, and an optional `refused` naming an `inviteId` and a reason (`no_sponsor`, `policy`, `limit`, `duplicate`, `no_public_url`). `attach_request` gains `account` (`kind`, `verified`) and, exactly when `via` is `invite`, an `invite` object with `inviteId`, the presented secret and the label. Roster entries gain `kind`, `inviteId` and `endsAt`. `revoke "*"` also cancels every live invite. A secret is 22 base64url characters (128 bits) drawn with `getRandomValues`; the adapter hashes its UTF-8 text with `crypto.subtle.digest('SHA-256')`, matching the relay's `digest()`. The relay keeps only the hash and forwards the presented secret.

**Accounts.** An invitee's `userId` is `g_` and the 32 hex characters of its account key, a prefix the config refuses for members. Its `displayName` is its verified email, cut to 97 characters and `...` past the 100-character name cap, or `unverified account`, and the widget always adds the key's first eight characters and an "invited" badge. When an invitee's name, after NFKC normalisation and case folding, equals a member's, the relay names it `invitee <short id>` instead. A dev-token entry may mark its user an invitee, outside public mode only, so the invitee-tier and `invite_required` tests need no provider; minting needs a public URL, so the invite tests and `demo:m4` run in public mode against `oauth2-mock-server`.

**Switch, sponsor and bounds.** `TABDOCK_INVITES`, off by default, turns invites on; off keeps M3's 403 for accounts off the allowlist, and on, the relay refuses to start when `usersPerPage` is 2 or less. The sponsor is the member attached longest when the invite is minted and stays fixed, because `/i` has shown that name; when the sponsor's attachment ends in any way, their invites end and so do the attachments those invites made. Every invite lasts at most 24 hours and 20 uses, and a page holds at most 10 live invites.

**Redemption.** `pair_page` takes exactly one of `code` or `invite` (the link or its secret, up to 300 characters) and accepts only an invite minted for one use, since a link pasted into a chat stays in its history; `invite` and `link` join the redacted log fields. Redemptions count against the pairing limits per user (10 a minute) and per page (30), and redemptions of one invite against 30 a minute, as previews of it already do; nothing on `/mcp` counts per address. `pair_page` and `/pair/claim` answer an invitee's pairing code with `invite_required` before the code is matched, so it is neither spent nor rotated. A member who is not attached and redeems an invite gets an invite-made attachment with its role cap, 24-hour end and seat rule; for someone already attached a redemption changes nothing. The relay grants an invite-made attachment the lesser of the invite's role and the decided role and refuses a `set_role` above the invite's role, and the adapter checks both again. A control invite redeemed while the driver seats are full raises a prompt saying the guest joins as observer, and the operator can promote them with `set_role` once a seat is free; three refusals or timeouts burn it. Revoking an invitee bars, for the invite's life, the digest of its `sub` and, when verified, of its email, because AuthKit deletes users fully and a new sign-up gets a new `sub`; a multi-use invite's Revoke defaults to "and close this link", and no redemption, an automatic watch approval included, clears a revoke.

**Adapter.** `policy.invites` is `off`, `watch` (default) or `all`. The control handle gains `invite({ label, role, lifetime, uses })`, which returns the link once. The adapter keeps each invite's id, secret hash, terms, refusal count and bars beside its grants under ADR 0011's key, never the secret, and drops them with the grants on `resumed: false`.

**SPEC wording.** Section 5: the User row reads "`userId`, `displayName`, `kind` (member or invitee) | Comes from the auth plugin; an invitee's id is `g_` plus a digest of its `sub`, and its name is its verified email or `unverified account` (ADR 0017)". The Attachment row adds `inviteId?` and `endsAt?`, noting "Created by an operator approval, under `autoApprove: 'observer'`, or by a live watch invite; an invite-made one ends at `endsAt`". The Invite row's note adds "at most 24 hours and 20 uses (control: 1), 10 live per page; the sponsor is the member attached longest at minting (ADR 0017)".

Section 6 gains `page to relay | invite_create | inviteId, role, label, uses, expiresAt, secretHash | Mint an invite; the relay keeps only the hash`, `page to relay | invite_cancel | inviteId | Cancel one invite` and `relay to page | invites | linkBase, invites[], refused? | After each welcome and on every change`. The `attach_request` payload reads "`requestId`, `user`, `account`, `via`, `invite?`, `client?`, `expiresAt`"; the `roster` payload "`attachments[]`, each with `kind`, `inviteId` and `endsAt`"; the `set_role`, `revoke` meaning "Operator controls; `revoke "*"` also cancels every live invite".

Section 7: the `pair_page` input reads "`code` or `invite` (a link minted for one use)", and the errors paragraph's second sentence reads "`invalid_arguments` covers arguments the relay will not forward: too large for one frame, or, from M2, failing the tool's `inputSchema` (ADR 0007)".

Section 8: the example policy adds `invites: 'watch'`; the widget paragraph ends "Only the code that called `attach()` holds the control handle, which from M4 also mints invites with `invite()`"; the lifecycle paragraph's last sentence reads "The resume token, the operator's grants, the page's invite records (never a secret) and the pause switch are kept per page, by relay URL plus the page's origin and path (ADR 0011)."

S14 reads: "Invites (from M4, ADRs 0016 and 0017): minted only while an allowlisted sponsor is attached and as far as `policy.invites` allows, at most 10 live per page, each for at most 24 hours and 20 uses; watch invites approve observers in advance; control invites prompt, allow one pending request and burn after three refusals or timeouts; the relay and the adapter grant no more than the invite's role, and the adapter honours an invite only against its own record and the presented secret; `revoke('*')` cancels every live invite; revoking an invitee bars that account and its verified email from the invite, a multi-use invite's Revoke closes the link by default, and no redemption clears a revoke; when a sponsor's attachment ends, so do its invites and the attachments they made; invite-made attachments end with the page session, on revoke or after 24 hours, and leave members two seats; a name that copies a member's shows the short id; redemption is limited per user, per invite and per page; `pair_page` and `/pair/claim` answer an invitee's pairing code with `invite_required`."

## Consequences

The adapter, not the relay, decides whether a presented secret is good, so a compromised relay cannot invent an invite or redeem one whose secret it has not seen; once a link has been redeemed, though, its remaining uses are only as safe as the relay, which the threat model records. Invite links die at every relay restart (ADR 0019). Each clause of the new S14 gets its own test.

## Notes from the interface review (4 October 2026)

Three reviewers, one per workstream, read the step 1 interfaces before the work split (`docs/plans/M4.md`). What they settled binds workstreams A and B; none of it changes the decision above.

**Clocks.** `invite_create` carries `expiresAt` on the page's clock, and the relay judges it on its own. One less than `MIN_INVITE_REMAINING_MS` (a minute) past the relay's now is refused with a sixth reason, `expired`; any other ends at the lesser of it and `createdAt` plus 24 hours, which is also where "while the page is open" ends. The `invites` frame lists the page's own requested value, null for open, so the adapter matches its record and shows its own clock's time, and the relay answers every `invite_create` with an `invites` frame that lists the invite or names it in `refused`, so `dock.invite()` always settles. A page clock running ahead stretches a short invite by the skew, never past 24 hours on the relay's clock; one running behind by more than the lifetime gets `expired`. The threat model, written in step 3, records both.

**What the adapter relies on the relay for.** Every redemption reaches the page as an `attach_request` with `via` `invite` and the presented secret, on a page with `autoApprove: 'observer'` and for a member redeeming alike: the relay never grants an invite-made attachment without the page's own answer, since the adapter honours an invite only against its record and the secret (S14) and never extends `autoApprove` to an invitee. Workstream A's suite tests this under `autoApprove: 'observer'`. A relay with invites off sends no `invites` frame at all, since no refusal reason means off; with them on it sends one right after every `welcome`, `linkBase` null when it has no public URL, and on every change. A `revoke` of one user bars them, and their verified email, from the invite that let them in but never cancels it: the page sends `invite_cancel` when the operator leaves "and close this link" checked, and `invite_cancel` ends no attachment the invite already made. `revoke "*"` still cancels every live invite.

**An invite on pair_page.** Text `inviteSecretOf` cannot read, the secret of an invite minted for more than one use, and an unknown, spent, cancelled or expired one all answer `pairing_expired` with one message, and are recorded as `attach_refused` with that outcome, so no answer says a secret is live.

**What outlives an invite.** An invite's record goes once it is used up, cancelled or expired, and a control invite is spent on approval, so the attachment it made keeps what its rules need. The relay's `AttachmentRecord` holds `inviteRole` (the invite's role, the cap `set_role` cannot pass), `sponsorId` and `emailHash` (the digest of an invitee's verified email, which a revoke bars); the adapter's stored grant holds `inviteRole` beside `inviteId` and `endsAt`, all three or none, the role never above it. Without them neither side could refuse a watch guest's promotion, promote a control guest who joined as observer, end a lost sponsor's attachments or bar a revoked email. The attach request keeps the token's `client_id` for the `attach` record an approval writes, and `pair_page` names the sponsor of an invite-made attachment, as `/i` does.

**Accounts.** The page link checks that `kind` is `invitee` exactly when the user id is an invitee's, on roster entries and attach requests, so a caller's kind follows from its id, and that a roster entry has `endsAt` exactly when it has `inviteId`. A dev-token entry whose id is `g_` and 32 lower-case hex characters is an invitee, named `unverified account` and refused unless `TABDOCK_INVITES` is on; any other id starting `g_` is refused in `TABDOCK_DEV_TOKENS` and `TABDOCK_OAUTH_USERS`. One function, `inviteeUser` in `auth.ts`, names every invitee for `/mcp`, `/pair` and `/i`: the plugin's token check and `BrowserSignIn.userOf` both use it, folding names by NFKC, case and invisible characters before comparing them with the members'.
