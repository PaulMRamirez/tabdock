# 0030: Limits and fixes from the last M4 review

Status: Accepted under the owner's standing instruction of 4 October 2026 (recorded 5 October 2026). Changes SPEC section 9 (S9); sections 7 and 11 name neither request-byte setting and are unchanged. Amends ADR 0018's notes: the charge for a waiting request is no longer called an upper bound, on the 2026-07-28 leg the request budget now counts `initialize`, `tools/list` and every other request, and with first-class tools on (ADR 0025) it counts a 2025-era `tools/list` too. This is decision D7 in `docs/notes/m5/m5-decisions.md`; the research, each item probed on branch `m5` in a scratch copy, is in `docs/notes/m5/backlog-fixes.md`.

## Context

After the last A4.3 pass a final hunt for high-severity issues found none and left two rows in `docs/plans/backlog.md` (5 October): five small items, each about availability or wording, and seven availability residuals to weigh against the threat model. In the scratch copy each small item failed a probe test before its fix and passed after, and the five fixes together passed 1,358 tests in 83 files.

The small items. `resolveConfig` checks each request-byte setting against `MIN_REQUEST_BYTES` but not against the other (`packages/relay/src/config.ts:819`), so `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES=8388608` starts beside the default 24 MiB per-user share, and since `relay.ts` answers 413 unparsed only past the share, a body charged between the two is parsed and then refused `page_busy`. `#approveRedemption` (`hub.ts:2364`) checks that the invite exists, the bar, the sponsor and the seats, never `expiresAt`: a guest who redeemed a control invite was attached when the operator allowed after the invite's end but before its timer ran. The adapter takes the relay's `deadlineMs` as any positive safe integer (`packages/adapter/src/core.ts:1839` and `1852`); Chromium 141 ran a 2^31 ms timer at once and wrapped 2^32 + 5,000 ms to 5 s, and Node runs such a timer after 1 ms, so a deadline past the timer maximum ends a call at once, and `holdUnwatched` (`core.ts:2153`) adds its 2 s grace and can overflow near the maximum, freeing the polyfill's write slot early (ADR 0012). `request-heap.ts:42` calls its charge an upper bound, but a 2026-07-28 call whose `_meta` held one key `"4294967294"` and then 190,000 short integer keys, which V8 keeps as dictionary elements, held 52.4 MB against 46.9 MB charged, 1.12 of it (0.85 on the 2025-era leg), against 0.94 for the worst shape ADR 0018's notes measured; the backlog's guess of about 5% was 12%. And `#sendInvoke` (`hub.ts:4265`) sent into a socket the page had begun to close: `#access` refuses a socket the relay is closing, but one the page closes stays in `CLOSING` until `ws` reports the close, up to `ws`'s 30 s `closeTimeout` if the peer keeps TCP open, and `ws` then sends nothing, so a `get_view` waited its 3 s deadline and wrote a full `call` line as if delivered (S7).

The residuals. `#holdBytes` (`hub.ts:2900`) ignores the account's kind, so three guests calling a page that stops reading (a frozen tab) hold 24 MiB each for 45 s at a time and members get `page_busy` everywhere. Node 22's `server.timeout` is 0, the MCP Node adapter waits for `drain` without limit, and the hub releases a call's charge before its response is written, so a client that stops reading leaves its response held, uncharged and with no end: a probe left 262,153 bytes queued with `writableNeedDrain` true indefinitely, and a member can do it 240 times a minute (a guest 60), each with up to 120,000 characters and a structured copy. On the 2026-07-28 leg only `tools/call` and `subscriptions/listen` spend the budget, so 50 `tools/list` passed a budget of 3 and `list_pages` still answered after them; each such request builds a server of its own (`createMcpFactory`, ADR 0025), and first-class page tools make each list heavier. A review of this record found the same gap in `tools/call`: one naming no registered tool, or whose params the SDK refuses before dispatch, gets the SDK's error in a 200 answer, which `refusedBySdk` (`relay.ts:261`, 4xx only) does not count, so it spends nothing either. The other four are smaller: a full `/pair` session store ends a guest's `/i` sign-in as readily as a stranger's (`pair.ts:454`); credential-free pages from a few addresses can fill most of the tool budget; the frames that pairing retries make the relay send a page count toward closing it on a very slow link; and an invitee email that looks like a member's name in another script passes `foldName` (`auth.ts:109`).

## Decision

**The five small items are fixed.**

_Request shares._ After the loop at `config.ts:819`, `resolveConfig` refuses a `requestBytesPerUser` above `requestBytes`, naming `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES_PER_USER` and `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES`; equal values pass, and `.env.example` says so. Test: `m4-interfaces.test.ts`, where the total alone at 8 MiB is refused naming the per-user setting.

_Expired invites._ After the guard at `hub.ts:2383`, an invite whose `expiresAt` is at or before now goes through `#closeInvite(invite, 'expired')`, and the request, if still open, ends `pairing_expired`, as the sponsor branch beside it does; nothing is granted under it. An invite's lifetime (S14) now holds at approval on the relay's own clock, not only through its timer and the adapter's record. Test: the probe, in the relay's `invites.test.ts`, asserting `invite_closed` with reason `expired` and no attachment.

_The relay's deadline in the adapter._ `MAX_TIMER_MS` moves from `config.ts:384` to `packages/protocol/src/constants.ts`, and the adapter uses `Math.min(frame.deadlineMs, MAX_TIMER_MS - UNWATCHED_HANDLER_GRACE_MS)` for both `deadlineAt` and its timer, so neither the deadline nor `holdUnwatched`'s grace passes the timer maximum. The schema keeps taking any positive safe integer, since refusing the frame would close the link over one odd deadline and reconnect into the next. Tabdock's own relay never sends more (`resolveConfig` already holds `callDeadlineMs` plus its grace within `MAX_TIMER_MS`); the cap is for a relay the adapter does not trust (threat model B5). Test: `core.test.ts`, where deadlines of 2^31 and `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` give no result after 1 s and `timeout` past the cap, and no delay over 2^31 - 1 reaches `timers.setTimeout`.

_The request charge._ `request-heap.ts` adds `INDEX_KEY_HEAP_BYTES`, 128 bytes, for each key made of digits alone: the scan marks a string of ASCII digits at its closing quote and adds the surcharge at the colon that follows (about 15 lines), which brought the shape above to 0.74 of its charge on the 2026-07-28 leg and 0.56 on the 2025-era leg. Wherever "upper bound" describes this charge (`request-heap.ts:42`, `config.ts:238` and `443`, `hub.ts:1012`, `2891` and `3727`, `.env.example`, ADR 0018's notes and threat model B1) it becomes a measure "set above what every shape in `call-shapes.ts` was measured to hold on both legs": a charge counted from bytes can be shown to cover the shapes tried, never every shape. Test: the shape joins `CALL_SHAPES` (`packages/relay/test/helpers/call-shapes.ts`), and `call-heap.test.ts`, which runs every shape on both legs, fails on the 2026-07-28 leg without the surcharge.

_A socket the page is closing._ `#sendInvoke` looks at the socket before encoding: one the relay is closing, or whose `readyState` is not `OPEN`, settles the call `page_asleep`, so the call writes a refusal line within the per-user refusal budget (S7) instead of a `call` line as if it had reached the page; the probe then answered in 12 ms. Test: the probe, in the relay's `page-link.test.ts`.

**Invitees hold at most a quarter of the request total.** `#holdBytes` takes the caller's kind and keeps one more sum, of what invitees' waiting requests hold. Invitees together may hold at most a quarter of `requestBytes`, or `MIN_REQUEST_BYTES` where a quarter is less, so one call of the largest arguments always fits: 16 MiB at the default 64 MiB, in every mode, with no new setting. The check runs after the per-user share and before the relay's total; past it a call is refused `page_busy` and a pairing or redemption `rate_limited`, the codes the total already uses, in words that say invited accounts hold all they may. This is the rank ADR 0016's notes give sessions, applied to memory: guests and strangers never hold more than a quarter, so guests calling a frozen tab can no longer leave members `page_busy` everywhere, while members, ranked above them, may still use the whole. Test: `request-bytes.test.ts`, where guests fill the quarter, the next guest call is refused and a member's is still held.

**A response the client leaves unread is cut off.** `relay.ts` keeps each `/mcp` response it hands either leg (`handleMcp`, `relay.ts:711`) in a set, with its user, until the response finishes or closes, and looks at the set every 10 s. A check that finds a response's `writableLength` above zero notes it; each later check that finds no less waiting counts one, and one that finds less, or none, starts over. At three, after at least 30 s and at most 40 s without progress, the relay destroys the response and writes a warn line naming the user, never a token or session id, once per kind a window through the `/mcp` repeated lines, the rest counted (ADR 0019's notes). That frees what the response held; a call still waiting on the response is cancelled as when its client goes, since the Node adapter aborts the request on close, and a 2025-era session or listen stream whose stream is cut can be opened again. As ADR 0024 rules for pages, a client that keeps reading is never cut: each read shrinks the queue, and only on a held stream, where the SDK adds a 13-byte keep-alive every 15 s, must a client read faster than about a byte a second. A response small enough for the kernel's socket buffers never shows here and costs only those buffers. Test: the check alone against a stand-in response under fake timers (on loopback the kernel absorbed about 3.5 MiB, more than one result, so a real socket shows nothing), and its wiring in `relay.ts`.

**Every 2026-07-28 request spends the request budget.** In `modern` (`relay.ts:510`), each JSON-RPC request the leg parses spends one request of its caller's budget before the SDK sees it, `tools/list`, `server/discover`, `ping` and an `initialize` that reaches that leg included, with two exceptions that spend elsewhere: a `subscriptions/listen`, which spends in `listen-streams.ts`, and a `tools/call` whose params pass the SDK's own `tools/call` request schema, which the relay's dispatcher (ADR 0025) is then sure to receive and which spends there, whatever name it carries, fixed, first-class, the spike's or unknown. A `tools/call` the SDK would refuse before dispatch, one with no `name` for instance, spends on arrival like any other request, so neither an unknown name nor malformed params make a request free, as both did in M4, each building a server through `createMcpFactory`. Past the budget it is answered 429 with a JSON-RPC error carrying the budget's own refusal and the request's id, as `sessions.ts` answers a user over the session cap; it gets no audit record, since like a refused listen it asks nothing of a page, and one line per kind a window. A request counts once: one that spent on arrival spends nothing more when the SDK then refuses it, while a body that is not one JSON-RPC request (a refused Content-Type, a body over the cap or not JSON, a batch, a posted response) still spends when the SDK refuses it, as now. A notification carries no request and spends nothing. On the 2025-era leg a session's server is built once and sessions are capped per user, so housekeeping stays unbudgeted there while first-class tools are off, as in M4. With them on, a member's list may hold 100,000 characters of page tools, so the relay's own `tools/list` handler (ADR 0025) spends one request of its caller's budget on that leg too and, past the budget, answers a JSON-RPC error carrying the budget's own refusal, with one line per kind a window and no audit record; with the flag off, that leg's housekeeping stays unbudgeted, as in M4. Tests: the probe becomes a test in `mcp-log-budget.test.ts`, and three of its tests take new counts; beside it, against a budget of 3, a run of `tools/call` with an unknown name and a run with no `name` are refused past the budget (`rate_limited` or 429) with `list_pages` refused after them, and with the flag on a run of 2025-era `tools/list` is refused past the budget too.

**Four residuals are accepted.** A full `/pair` session store (200) ends the oldest session of an account that is not a member first, and a guest partway through `/i` holds such a session, looking like any stranger until it redeems; that costs a guest a fresh sign-in, as B2 already accepts six addresses stalling `/i` for everyone, and never ends a member's session. A page's tool list is charged at most about 5.36 MB (the `relisted` shape), so about 12 pages fill the 64 MiB tool budget, from three addresses in hosted mode and one elsewhere, after which any page that lists its tools again is closed with 1008; that is B3's class at a lower price, and ranking pages with a member attached goes to the backlog. The frames that pairing retries make the relay send a page count toward ADR 0024's 256, but pairing is capped at 30 attempts a minute per page, so 256 take over eight minutes of a link that accepts nothing, long after the 30 s silence close. And `foldName` folds NFKC, case and invisible characters, not cross-script look-alikes, which need Unicode's confusables data (a new dependency or a vendored table); prompts and the roster still show the invited badge and short id, as S14 asks. The threat model records each (B2, B4, B5).

**Also from the hunt.** Tracked files hold no TODO, FIXME or HACK. The nine "After the merge" items in `docs/checklists/M4.md` are due now that M4 has merged, and M5's first commit expands them into owner steps in `docs/checklists/M5.md`, the proxy range first among the steps after the first deploy, since every per-address limit in B3 rests on it. The deploy snapshot keeps its backlog row ("M5, if re-pairing annoys"), since the owner has not reported re-pairing as a nuisance.

**SPEC wording.** S9 gains three sentences after "and, behind a host edge, page sessions and sign-ins per client address (ADR 0018).": "Requests that may wait on a page (calls, and pairings waiting for the operator) are charged what their bodies are measured to hold on the heap, within a share per user and a total for the relay that the share may not exceed; invitees together hold at most a quarter of the total, or one call of the largest arguments where a quarter is less (ADRs 0018 and 0030). On the 2026-07-28 leg every request counts once against its user's requests per minute, `tools/list`, `server/discover`, `initialize` and a `tools/call` for an unknown tool included, and while first-class tools are on so does every 2025-era `tools/list` (ADR 0025). An `/mcp` response that a client leaves unread, so that what waits to be sent stops shrinking for 30 s, is cut off and logged; a client that keeps reading is never cut (ADR 0030)." ADR 0025 rewrites an earlier phrase of the same item, and ADR 0026 widens this one's first phrase to requests that may wait on a client's confirmation; none of the three edits overlaps another. Sections 7 and 11 name neither `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES` nor `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES_PER_USER`, so neither changes; the new refusal is S9's "a total for the relay that the share may not exceed" and a line in `.env.example`.

**ADR 0018.** Its notes gain a paragraph from this record: the request charge is a measure set above every shape measured, with the integer-key surcharge, and the earlier notes' "still never `initialize` or `tools/list`" holds for the 2025-era leg only, and there only while first-class tools are off.

## Consequences

No section 9 requirement is weakened. S9 gains three limits and at last names the request-byte limits ADR 0018's notes built; S7's records grow more accurate, since a call into a socket the page is closing no longer counts as reached; S14's invite lifetimes hold at approval on the relay's clock; the adapter's distrust of the relay gains the deadline cap; S5, S6, S8 and S13 are untouched.

Four changes are visible. A `.env` that lowers `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES` below the 24 MiB default share no longer starts; lower `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES_PER_USER` with it. A 2026-07-28 client now spends a request on each discover and list: Claude Code sends one `server/discover` and one `tools/list` when it connects and lists again on each change, at most once a second under ADR 0025's notifier, so a member's 240 a minute leaves room for calls, and a client that lists faster than its `ttlMs` spends its own budget; the relay that ADR 0027's conformance run starts raises `TABDOCK_MAX_REQUESTS_PER_USER` for that run alone, beside its session cap, since that run passes 240 requests a minute. A client that stops reading a response for 30 s loses it, and a call it still waited on ends as if it had left; one that reads never does. And guests' waiting requests share 16 MiB, so on a page many guests call with large arguments a guest meets `page_busy` before a member would.

What is left. The request charge remains a measure: a shape nobody has tried may hold more than its charge, as the integer keys did before the surcharge. The tool budget's charge (`hub.ts:874`, ADR 0018's notes) is still called an upper bound and counts keys the same way; keys of digits alone were not measured against it, and the backlog takes that check. The cut-off bounds how long an unread response is held, not how many: until it, a member can leave about 160 unread (240 a minute for 40 s) and a guest about 40, each up to 120,000 characters and its structured copy, in memory no budget counts; this was not measured against the reference host's 512 MB, and the backlog keeps a measurement and, if it matters, a per-user cap on responses with data waiting. The four accepted residuals stand as the threat model records them. Tests: `m4-interfaces.test.ts`, the relay's `invites.test.ts` and `page-link.test.ts`, the adapter's `core.test.ts`, `call-heap.test.ts` with `call-shapes.ts`, `request-bytes.test.ts`, `mcp-log-budget.test.ts`, and the stall check's own test.

## Notes from M5 Step 1 (5 October 2026)

The build follows the decision and settles three details it left open. The scan counts a key of digits and escapes as digits alone, since an escape such as `\u0031` parses to a digit; that can only overstate, and an empty key is never one. The quarter refuses in the words "requests from invited accounts already hold all of the relay's memory they may while they wait". The cut-off lives in `response-stalls.ts`, whose test, `response-stalls.test.ts`, runs the check against stand-in responses and its wiring against one marked request whose response reports bytes waiting; the adapter's test of `holdUnwatched` near the timer maximum sits in `operator.test.ts` beside its siblings, and the shape is `metaIndexKeys`. A batch or a refused Content-Type still spends when the SDK refuses it, while a posted response or a body that is not JSON goes to the 2025-era leg, as in M4.

## Notes from the Step 1 review (5 October 2026)

ADR 0032 changes three things above. Every 2026-07-28 JSON-RPC request but a listen now spends as it arrives, `tools/call` included, and a fixed tool spends nothing more for a request already paid; only a call past the budget that names one of the five fixed tools, with a `requestState` the SDK will not refuse, goes on to be refused in that tool's words with its record. That closes what the Step 1 notes above deferred: an unknown name, and a `requestState` that is a number or `null`, which the SDK refuses before any handler, each spent nothing. The cut-off measures progress by the socket's finished bytes, `bytesWritten` less `writableLength`, and `relay.ts` writes every answer in 16 KiB slices so they move, since one large write showed none until its last byte went out and a client reading steadily was cut. And each invitee holds at most a quarter of the invitees' pool, at least `MIN_REQUEST_BYTES`, so one guest no longer fills it, with invites refused at start under a total of 8 MiB. The Consequences' "at most once a second" is now once every 10 s (ADR 0032), so four clients relisting on every change spend 24 of a member's 240 requests a minute, not all of them.

## Notes from M5 Step 2 (5 October 2026)

The relay's own dispatcher (ADR 0025, `mcp.ts`) now spends one request for every `tools/call` on the 2025-era leg, before it resolves the name, so a name the relay does not serve costs a request there too and, past the budget, is answered a JSON-RPC error carrying the budget's refusal with one line a window and no record; and while first-class tools are on, its `tools/list` handler spends one request for every 2025-era `tools/list`, answered the same way past the budget. On the 2026-07-28 leg both spent as they arrived (ADR 0032), and the dispatcher spends nothing more there. `mcp-log-budget.test.ts` holds both, with the flag on and off.
