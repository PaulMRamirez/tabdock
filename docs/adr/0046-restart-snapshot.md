# 0046: Restart snapshot

Status: Accepted, 10 October 2026, under the owner's standing instruction. Brought in by ADR 0043 from the backlog row of 3 October (option B of D3 in `docs/notes/m4/m4-decisions.md`), which asked for a record of its own. Changes SPEC sections 4 (what persists), 9 (S7 and S9) and 11 (a host's graceful stop), and adds A6.18 to section 10. Amends ADR 0019's rule that a restart ends every page session, and adds notes to ADRs 0017 and 0019 and to D3.

## Context

The relay keeps pages, attachments, invites and, from M6, time-boxed sessions in memory, so every restart, a planned deploy included, ends them all and every page pairs again (ADR 0019). D3 weighed three choices on 3 October and took (A), the audit log alone on disk, noting that (B), a snapshot written at a graceful stop and loaded once at the next start, could follow if re-pairing proved a nuisance. ADR 0043 makes it one: a teacher who deploys during a lesson would detach the whole class.

Two things make a snapshot dangerous. A stale or replayed file could bring back an attachment that was revoked, or an invite that was closed, after the snapshot was taken (S8, S14). And `docs/deploy.md`'s credential rotation (its steps at lines 103 and 220) relies on a restart ending every attachment, so a snapshot on by default would quietly undo that procedure.

## Options

A. No snapshot: every restart ends everything, as in M5.

B. A snapshot on by default wherever there is an audit directory, written at a graceful stop and loaded once within 10 minutes (ADR 0043's design).

C. B, but off unless the operator turns it on, and loaded only when the audit log's hash chain shows it was the relay's last act before its stop.

## Decision

C. `TABDOCK_RESTART_SNAPSHOT` is a flag, off by default, refused at start without an audit directory (local mode's counts). With it on:

- **Writing.** At a graceful stop, after the hub stops taking new work and before `relay_stop`, the relay writes one file into the audit directory at mode 0600, through a temporary file renamed into place and synced, as the owner token is written. It holds pages (origin, path, title, policy and the session ceiling, any time-boxed session, the resume token's digest), attachments, live invites with their digests, terms, sponsors and bars, and agent tokens' records, never a secret, token or code. The `snapshot_written` audit record carries counts and the file's SHA-256, and `relay_stop` follows it.
- **Loading.** At the next start, before it listens, the relay reads the file and unlinks it before parsing; an unlink that fails means no load. It applies the snapshot only if its `writtenAt` is under `RESUME_WINDOW_MS` (10 minutes) old and not in the future, it parses strictly at `SNAPSHOT_VERSION`, and the audit log's last two records are `snapshot_written` naming that digest followed by `relay_stop`. Anything else is a fresh start, as without the setting.
- **What comes back.** Pages return asleep with a fresh resume window, so their tabs resume as after a dropped link. Attachments of users who are no longer valid (a member no longer listed, ADR 0043) are dropped and counted. Timers for idle expiry, invites, agent tokens and sessions are re-armed from their stored ends, and a session already past its end ends at once with reason `time`. `snapshot_loaded` records the age and counts, the dropped included.

A crash writes nothing, so it still ends everything. The guide and `docs/deploy.md` say to leave the setting off while rotating credentials.

## Consequences

A planned deploy within 10 minutes keeps a lesson: pages resume, guests stay attached, and invite links still work. Binding the file to the audit chain means a file planted or kept from an earlier run never loads, and unlinking before applying means it loads at most once, so a revoke or a closed invite after the write cannot be undone by it. Emails (as invitees' display names) and page titles sit on disk for up to 10 minutes, at 0600 in the audit directory, beside an audit log that already names them. Fly's `kill_timeout` must leave room for the write; the owner's checklist measures the gap between SIGTERM and `relay_stop`.

## Notes from the build (11 October 2026)

**The file.** `relay-snapshot.json` in the audit directory, read and written against the protocol's `RelaySnapshotSchema` (`packages/protocol/src/snapshot.ts`), whose every object is strict and whose references are checked whole: each page, invite and token once, no digest twice, and a sponsor still attached for everything it sponsors (S14). The stop checks its own snapshot against the schema before writing, so it never leaves a file a start would refuse, and writes nothing past `MAX_SNAPSHOT_BYTES` (16 MiB), logging that as an error, since the next start then ends everything. An invitee keeps only the clients its roster row shows (`MAX_ROSTER_CLIENTS_PER_INVITEE`), which brings a hosted relay's fullest store at its defaults, every seat taken and every name at its longest, to about 12.1 MB; with all twenty it would pass 24 MB and never be written. The policy in force is not stored: a start works it out again from the ceiling and the session, so a session can only narrow (S5). A token that was asking comes back dormant; pending requests, proposals, published state, pairing codes and QR nonces are not carried. Restored pages are counted under one stand-in address, so no client address is ever charged for them, and the relay-wide page sessions count them as asleep, first to make way.

**Who comes back.** The decision's "a member no longer listed" is held more tightly: each member's attachment carries a digest of the subject its user id stood for at the stop (`memberKey`, never the subject), and loads only when the member list in force gives that id the same subject, so an id removed, or handed to someone else while the relay was down, restores nothing (S8, S13), as a live list change ends it. An invitee whose subject the list now names as a member is dropped too, since that subject signs in as the member. Those who stay are named as the list in force names them, by the hub's own renaming rule, as a live change would: a member renamed while the relay was down under the new name, and an invitee whose name now folds to a member's under its short id (ADR 0017). Each drop runs the hub's own ends, so the log holds the `expire` (membership_changed) records, the sponsor's invites closing, what those let in ending and `sponsor_gone`, then `snapshot_loaded` counting the attachments dropped. A stop while a member list change is still owed (one whose settling threw part way, which `settlingMembers` settles again with the next apply) writes no snapshot and logs why: the new list is in force, but what it took away has not ended, so keys taken from it would hand a moved id's attachments to the id's new holder. Dev tokens and local mode keep no member list, so their members return as they were; production always has one, and the guide says to rotate dev tokens with the setting off. A snapshot is refused whole, rather than cut, when written in another mode (a user id names one person only within one mode's accounts), or when it would put back what this relay could not have let in: more page sessions than it holds, a page from an origin it no longer allows (whose tab could never resume), invites or invitees with invites off, agent tokens with them off, or a page whose attachments its seats could not have let in. Seats are counted as the hub counts them, and only what the hub holds to at every moment is asked: the people within `usersPerPage`, those an invite let in among them leaving members their reserve, and the watchers a watch invite or an agent token let in within the watching seats. A guest an operator moved down from the people may watch past the seats until someone leaves (ADR 0044), so the watchers in all are not held to them; a limit lowered while the relay was down refuses the rest (S9).

**The binding.** The start reads the newest audit files' last lines before writing `relay_start`, across a rotation between the two, and requires the last to be `relay_stop` whose `seq` and `prev` chain it to the `snapshot_written` before it, which names the file's SHA-256. The hub refuses `detach_page` from the moment it stops taking work ("the relay is shutting down"), and the stop exports the store only after every page socket has closed, then writes the file, `snapshot_written` and `relay_stop` with nothing awaited between them, so nothing can end an attachment between the read and the log's last line, and a detach cannot come back. Unlinking is followed by a best-effort directory sync; were a power loss to bring the file back, the synced `relay_start` after it still ends the binding. Each refusal names its reason in fixed words, never a value from the file.

**Credentials.** The decision leaves rotation to the operator, who turns the setting off first; the build also binds the file to the credentials it was written under, so a rotation that forgets ends everything anyway. The file holds a domain-separated SHA-256 of the `/pair` client (`credentials`, null without one, never the secret), and a start whose `/pair` client differs refuses it whole, as `docs/deploy.md`'s rotation after a suspected leak replaces that secret. No auth plugin exposes its dev tokens or issuer to the relay, so rotating those still needs the setting off, as the guide says.

**Off means gone.** A start with the setting off removes a snapshot an earlier run left, unread, so emails and titles never wait on disk for a later start. A stop killed between writing its temporary file and renaming it into place (a host's stop timeout ending a slow stop, say) leaves `.relay-snapshot.json.<16 hex>.tmp`, which nothing reads: every start removes such files unread, whatever the setting, never following a link, and logs how many. A relay stopped for good with the setting on leaves its file until a next start: delete `relay-snapshot.json` from the audit directory when retiring one.

**Plan risk 4.** The loader refuses an unbound or stale snapshot soundly, so the setting ships, off by default, in M6 rather than moving to M7. `restart-snapshot.test.ts` holds A6.18, and each of its security cases was checked to fail with the code it covers removed.

## Notes from Wave 3's cross review (12 October 2026)

**A file that cannot be removed.** "Off means gone" can fail: a snapshot an earlier run left on a read-only mount, or one another account owns, survives the start's unlink with invitees' emails and page titles in it. The start logged that at info level as "removed a restart snapshot an earlier run left, unread", contradicted only by its `removed: false` field, so an operator who turned the setting off to keep names off the disk was told the file had gone. Now, whichever way the setting is, a snapshot the start could not remove logs one warning, "restart snapshot left on disk and not loaded; remove it by hand", with the fixed reason ("it could not be removed (EISDIR)") and `removed: false`, and SPEC section 4 says a start with the setting off warns where it cannot remove the file. `restart-snapshot.test.ts` holds the level and the words with the setting off and on, a directory at the snapshot's name standing in for a file unlink cannot remove, since `createRelay` takes no filesystem; it fails without the change. Nothing is loaded either way, as before.

**Restored pages and the per-address limit.** A snapshot names no client address, so the hub counted every page it put back under one stand-in address, and a resume never moved a page's count. After a restart, then, an address that held its `pageSessionsPerAddress` pages (5 in hosted mode, ADR 0018) held none on the books: it could open that many again while its restored tabs resumed and stayed, and each restart within the resume window added another allowance, up to the relay-wide `pageSessions`. Now a restored page's first resume charges it to the address it resumes from, under that address's own limit as a new page session would be: past it the address's own page asleep longest ends, and with none asleep the resume is refused with 1013 and the page stays asleep and resumable until its window ends. Every later resume leaves the count where it is, as before. SPEC's S9 says so, and `restart-snapshot.test.ts` holds both branches: two restored pages that resume leave no room for a third new one, which fails without the change, and a restored page finding the address full waits until one of the address's own pages sleeps and is ended for it.

**A fresh window, said where the window is.** The Decision gives every restored page a fresh resume window, and the snapshot stores no `asleepAt`, so a tab closed nine minutes before a deploy keeps its page, and the attachments on it, for the downtime (under 10 minutes, or the snapshot does not load) and a new 10 minutes, and each graceful restart may extend that again. SPEC section 6 still stated the 10 minute window without the exception and section 4 did not mention it. Both now say a start from the snapshot gives every page it carries a fresh window however long it had slept, as guide page 14 does. The Decision stands, since only the page's own life is extended: each attachment's idle expiry, each invite's and agent token's end and each session's end are re-armed from their stored ends, revoke and the member list still end what they end, and only an operator's graceful restart extends anything. Saving `asleepAt` and restoring only the window left would change the Decision, so it is left to the owner.

**How long a stop takes, and Fly's `kill_timeout`.** The Consequences left the write's length to the owner's checklist, while `fly.toml` and guide page 07 said the relay closes within a second. Measured in the sandbox on 12 October (`docs/notes/verified.md`), stopping a hub that holds the fullest hosted store and writing its 12 MB snapshot takes 0.3 to 0.5 s at a whole CPU and 6 to 9 s at shared-cpu-1x's baseline share, past the 5 s `kill_timeout` that Fly honours only as best effort. A stop cut short writes no snapshot and the next start ends everything, so nothing is weakened, but the setting would fail when a relay is fullest. `fly.toml` now sets `kill_timeout = 30` (Fly allows 300), its comment, the Dockerfile's, guide page 07 and `docs/deploy.md` give the figures, and `restart-snapshot.test.ts` holds the work's CPU under `kill_timeout` times the baseline share, which fails at 5. The checklist's measurement on Fly stands.
