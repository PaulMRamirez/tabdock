# 0024: A page that stops reading

Status: Accepted, 4 October 2026, from the final A4.3 pass, under the owner's standing instruction to take the recommended option. Changes SPEC section 6's close codes and S9's list of limits.

## Context

`/page` takes no credential, and the relay answers some page frames with frames of its own: a `ping` with a `pong`, a `revoke` with a roster and, with invites on, the page's invites, an `invite_cancel` or a refused `invite_create` with the invites, `rotate_pairing` with a `pairing`, and `ws` answered every WebSocket ping control frame with a pong by itself. `hub.ts` sent each answer with `ws.send` and never looked at what the socket still held, and nothing closed a socket whose peer had stopped reading: its idle timer is refreshed by any frame it sends. So a script that said `hello`, paused its own reading and flooded such frames left every answer queued in the relay's memory. The final A4.3 pass measured it with the relay in a process of its own: a million pings held 80 to 140 MiB of heap, a million revokes of everyone about 360 MiB, and a million WebSocket pings with 125-byte payloads 275 MiB of heap and 125 MiB of buffers, each from one socket and no account, past the reference host's 512 MB. That ends every page and attachment on the relay and leaves the audit log a gap (ADR 0019). The hole predates ADR 0023, which nonetheless leaned on it ("a socket can already send pings without limit") and widened it by keeping open sockets that sent frames that change nothing, each of which may be answered.

## Decision

**What the relay holds for a page.** Before each frame it sends a page, and before each pong to a WebSocket ping, the hub looks at the socket's own queue (`bufferedAmount`: the bytes Node has not yet handed to the kernel, which stays empty while the page reads). Frames sent while that queue is not empty are counted, and the count starts over whenever it is empty again. Past 256 such frames (`MAX_UNREAD_FRAMES`), or once a frame would take the queue past 2 MiB, two frames of the largest size (`MAX_UNREAD_BYTES`), the page is not reading what it asks for: the socket is closed with 1008, reason `page is not reading`, and cut off after the usual 2 s, which frees what it held. The page sleeps and may resume, as after any close. Frames are counted as well as bytes because each small frame queued costs a few hundred bytes of bookkeeping beside its own.

**WebSocket pings.** The relay's `WebSocketServer` runs with `autoPong: false`, and the hub answers each ping control frame itself, once, within the same bound.

**SPEC wording.** Section 6's close-code paragraph reads "... and uses 1001, 1008 and 1009 for idle or shutdown, malformed frames (a first frame that is not `hello` among them, ADR 0023, and a page or address over its `tools`-frame budget), a page that leaves more of what the relay sends unread than the relay holds for it (ADR 0024) and oversized frames, and 1013 when there is no room for a new page session (ADR 0012)." S9's list gains "what the relay holds unread for one page" after "the memory page tool lists may hold".

## Consequences

One socket now costs the relay at most about 2 MiB of queued frames, or 256 small ones, beyond what the kernel holds for it, however it floods; the kernel's own buffers are bounded per socket by the host. A page that reads is never closed for this: a reading page empties the queue as fast as the relay fills it, and one frame of the largest size always fits. A page whose browser stops reading for a while (a frozen tab) is closed at most a little sooner than its idle timeout would have closed it, and resumes like after any close. Tests: `page-backpressure.test.ts` (a paused page flooding pings, revokes of everyone, `invite_cancel`, `rotate_pairing` and WebSocket pings, each closed with the relay's heap and buffers within 16 MiB of where they started, and a reading page sending 20,000 pings never closed), `hub.test.ts` (the frame count, its reset, the byte cap and WebSocket pings at their exact edges).

## Notes from the last A4.3 pass (4 October 2026)

A skeptic re-checked this record's fix. These notes replace the sentences above that they contradict.

**Invokes.** The hub sent each call's invoke with `ws.send` and never through the bound, so invokes were neither checked nor closed anything: with the relay in its own process at its defaults, a page that paused its reading while a member sent read-only calls of 1 MB held 56.5 MiB queued on its socket after 60 calls and 116.1 MiB after 120, with no close, and under the image's `--max-old-space-size=192` the relay ran out of heap at 60 calls where a page that read but never answered lasted to 80. Worse, the next small frame then found the queue past 2 MiB and closed the page as not reading although it read everything: over a link carrying 2 to 4 MiB a second, an observer's burst of eight or ten large calls and one cancel, or the relay's own heartbeat ping, closed a page that had received every invoke, failing the operator's call and most of the observer's with `page_asleep`. So this record itself had let an observer, a watch invitee among them, end the operator's page, and its claims that "a page that reads is never closed for this" and that "one frame of the largest size always fits" did not hold.

**What changed.** An invoke now goes out only onto an empty queue, or while the queue with it stays within `INVOKE_ROOM`, one frame of the largest size (1 MiB), and fewer than 256 frames are queued. Otherwise the call waits for room on that socket, behind any call already waiting, so calls keep their order and a write keeps its place at the head of its page's queue; its deadline runs on, and one that never gets room ends with `timeout` saying it never reached the page. Each frame the hub hands `ws` is now counted until `ws` reports it handed to the kernel (its send callback), so the counts are of frames still queued rather than of frames sent while the queue was not empty; each such report is the page making progress and sends the calls waiting for room. Invokes and cancels never close a page: a cancel follows at most one invoke that went out, so the call limits bound them. Every other frame, the answers to what the page sends among them, is checked as before: past 256 of them still queued, or once the queue in all would pass `MAX_UNREAD_BYTES` (2 MiB), the page is closed with 1008. Since invokes hold at most half of that, a page that reads, however slowly, is closed only when the frames other than calls it is sent pile up unread past the bound, and the relay's other frames are far smaller than the room invokes leave.

**What a page costs now.** A socket holds at most about `MAX_UNREAD_BYTES` of queued frames, or 256 that count toward closing it, beyond what the kernel takes, whoever sends what (cancels, at most one an invoke, are the only frames not checked against it); the calls waiting for room hold only what their own requests hold, which ADR 0018's notes bound per user and for the relay. A page that stops reading is no longer closed for calls sent to it: its calls wait and end at their deadline, and the page, if it sends nothing, ends at its idle timeout, or, if it keeps sending frames the relay answers, is closed for those as before. Tests: `hub.test.ts` (invokes held past `INVOKE_ROOM` and sent in order as a page reads one frame at a time; a held call cancelled or timed out without telling the page; a held call ended at once by a revoke (S8) and a held write refused once its caller is demoted (S5), neither reaching the page; forty cancels with the counted frames one short of the cap closing nothing; the frame count of frames still queued), `page-backpressure.test.ts` (thirty 1 MB calls to a paused page with at most 0.95 MiB queued and no close, where 24.8 MiB were queued before; a page reading through a 2 MiB a second proxy, with the relay's heartbeat every 2 s and an observer's ten 1 MB calls and a cancel, never closed, every call answered, where before it was closed and most calls failed).
